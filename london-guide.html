How to Live on £50/Week in London

Student Hacks  |  6 min read  |  Updated 2026

London has a reputation for eating your money, and it is fair. But thousands of students get by here every year. In this guide, £50 a week means everything except rent and bills, which your maintenance loan or wages should cover separately. It is tight, but it is doable with a plan.

Start with the honest maths

Before any tips, give every pound a job. Here is one way to split £50:

CategoryWeekly budget
Food and drink£25
Travel£10
Phone and subscriptions£5
Fun and social£7
Essentials and buffer£3

Treat these as a starting point and adjust them to your life. Our savings calculator will show you how the numbers add up.

Housing hacks that protect your budget

Rent is the biggest cost, so small choices here matter more than any coupon.

  • Look beyond Zones 1 and 2. Rooms in outer zones are often noticeably cheaper, and a good bus or rail link can make up the difference.
  • Choose bills-included rooms. They make your weekly spending predictable, especially through winter.
  • Claim your council tax exemption. Full-time students are generally exempt. Ask your university for a certificate and send it to the council so you are not billed by mistake.
  • Check the paperwork. Your deposit should be protected in a government-approved scheme, and you should never pay fees you were not told about.

Eat well on around £25 a week

Food is where most students overspend, and also where you can save the most.

  • Shop at discount supermarkets. Aldi and Lidl are usually cheaper on basics like pasta, rice, eggs and frozen veg.
  • Hunt for reduced items. Many supermarkets mark down fresh food in the evening. Freeze what you will not eat that day.
  • Use food-sharing apps. Too Good To Go and Olio can get you surplus food at a fraction of the price or for free.
  • Batch cook. A big pot of chilli, dal or pasta sauce becomes four lunches. Share a cooking night with housemates to split costs.
  • Skip the daily meal deal. Taking your own lunch can save a surprising amount over a term.

A simple day could be porridge for breakfast, a homemade wrap for lunch and rice with veg and beans for dinner. It is not glamorous, but it keeps you full and on budget.

Get around for less

  • Get a 16-25 Railcard if you travel by train, as it takes a third off many fares.
  • Apply for a student photocard. If you are 18 or over, a TfL student Oyster photocard gives a discount on Travelcards and Bus and Tram Passes.
  • Use the bus. With the Hopper fare, you can make unlimited bus and tram journeys within an hour for one fare.
  • Walk or cycle short trips. London is more walkable than it looks, and it costs nothing.

Free and cheap fun

You do not need to spend big to enjoy London. Many major museums and galleries are free, and the city has huge parks, markets and free events. Sign up for UNiDAYS or Student Beans for discounts, and check your students' union for cheap tickets and society freebies.

Stay in control

Set a weekly spending limit and track it in a banking app with spending pots. Check your balance every few days, not just at the end of the month, so surprises do not pile up. If you overspend one week, adjust the next rather than giving up.

The bottom line

Living on £50 a week in London takes planning and a bit of discipline, but it is realistic. Keep your housing costs under control, cook most meals yourself, use every student discount you can and watch your spending weekly. Do that and you will graduate with experience, not just debt.
