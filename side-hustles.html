Top 5 UK Student Side Hustles

Side Hustles  |  7 min read  |  Updated 2026

Between lectures, deadlines and a social life, finding time to earn can feel impossible. The good news is that the best student side hustles are flexible, low-cost to start and fit around your timetable. Here are five worth considering, followed by a plain-English guide to tax.

1. Tutoring

If you are strong in a subject, someone will pay for your help. Many students tutor GCSE and A-level pupils in maths, English, sciences or languages, either in person or online. Rates vary widely depending on the subject, level and your experience, so start modestly and raise your price as you gather good reviews.

Tip: Ask for an enhanced DBS check through a tutoring agency if you will work with under-18s, as many parents expect it.

2. Freelancing

Writing, graphic design, video editing, social media management and web design can all be done from your bedroom. Build a small portfolio, even with practice projects, and pitch to small businesses and student societies. Freelancing takes longer to get going than other options, but it can pay well once you have regular clients.

3. Hospitality, events and gig work

Bar, restaurant and event staffing are popular because shifts are often evening or weekend based. Delivery and similar gig work lets you choose your hours entirely. Check what you would really earn after costs such as fuel, bike maintenance or equipment, and make sure you understand whether you are an employee or self-employed.

4. Campus jobs and ambassador roles

Universities regularly hire students as library assistants, open day guides, student ambassadors and research helpers. These roles usually work around your timetable and often pay at least the National Minimum Wage for your age, which you can check on gov.uk. They also look good on a CV.

5. Selling things you no longer need

Clearing out clothes, books and gadgets on Vinted, eBay or Facebook Marketplace is an easy way to raise cash. Selling your own unwanted items is generally not taxable. However, buying items to sell on for profit is treated as trading, and different rules apply.

What about tax?

Many students worry about this, so here are the basics. Rules and thresholds can change, so always check the latest figures on gov.uk.

  • Personal Allowance: Most people can earn up to a set amount each tax year before paying Income Tax. At the time of writing this is £12,570.
  • Trading Allowance: If you earn up to £1,000 a year in gross self-employed income, you usually do not need to tell HMRC or pay tax on it.
  • Above £1,000: You may need to register for Self Assessment. You generally need to register by 5 October following the end of the tax year, and file your return by 31 January after that.
  • Employed work: Jobs through a payroll are taxed through PAYE. If too much tax is taken, you can often claim a refund.
  • Online platforms: Some platforms now report seller earnings to HMRC, so keep accurate records of what you earn.
  • Student loans: Repayments depend on your income after you finish your course, not on part-time earnings while you study.
  • International students: Your visa may limit the hours or type of work you can do, so check the conditions before you start.

Getting started

Pick one hustle that matches your skills and your free time, rather than trying everything at once. Track what you earn and spend, and set aside a little for tax if you are self-employed. Use our savings calculator to see how much extra income could change your weekly budget.
